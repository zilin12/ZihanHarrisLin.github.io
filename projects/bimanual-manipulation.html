Learning-Based Bimanual Manipulation

An investigation into simulation, perception, and robot learning for manipulation policies that require fewer manually collected demonstrations.

Motivation & research questions

Collecting demonstrations for every new manipulation task can limit the accessibility and reuse of robot-learning systems. This project explores a flexible pipeline for training policies across different tasks.

  • How can simulation-generated data reduce dependence on human demonstrations?
  • How can perception, motion planning, and learned policies be combined for bimanual tasks?
  • Can task generation and curriculum design make policy development more reusable?

Approaches under consideration

  • Simulation-based policy development and simulated demonstration generation.
  • Vision-language-action policies, including models such as π₀.
  • Reinforcement learning and task-oriented learning.
  • RGB-D object perception, segmentation, and motion planning.
  • Task generation and experimental benchmarking.

Platforms & tools being explored

ALOHA-style bimanual robots, LeRobot, MuJoCo, PyBullet, RGB-D perception, and vision-language-action models are under consideration. These are candidate technologies rather than a claim that every component has been implemented or validated.

Role & project context

Undergraduate engineering capstone at Purdue University. I serve as project manager and schedule manager, supporting the planning and coordination of the manipulation investigation.

Progress & limitations

The project is ongoing. Reduced demonstration requirements, cross-task reuse, and greater data efficiency are objectives under investigation.

No proposed performance target is presented as an achieved result. Hardware selection, final methods, and experimental findings will be documented as they are confirmed.

Project materials

Repository links, platform photographs, demonstrations, and benchmark results will be added when available.
