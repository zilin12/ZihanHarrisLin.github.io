Development project

Vision-Based Robotic Excavator Control

A vision-based control and state-estimation framework for a small-scale hydraulic excavator, estimating joint configurations without physical joint encoders.

Motivation & research questions

Camera observations offer a way to estimate a robot’s configuration when joint encoders are unavailable. This project connects visual measurements to the excavator’s kinematic model.

  • How can observations of tagged mechanical components be translated into joint configurations?
  • How do visual measurement errors affect geometric consistency and state estimates?
  • How can camera-derived state information be integrated into a ROS 2 robotic system?

Technical methods

  • AprilTag-based localization of mechanical components from camera observations.
  • Kinematic modeling to relate observed component poses to robot configuration.
  • Joint-state estimation without physical joint encoders.
  • ROS 2 integration of visual state information.
  • Investigation of measurement error and geometric consistency.

Hardware & software

Small-scale hydraulic excavator, camera observations, AprilTag tracking, ROS 2, and robot kinematic models.

Results & limitations

The project work covers camera-based configuration estimation and its integration with robot modeling and control. Quantitative accuracy, control-performance benchmarks, and a confirmed completion date are not available here.

Visual measurement error and geometric consistency remain important considerations. No numerical performance or deployment result is claimed.
