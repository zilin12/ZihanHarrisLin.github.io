Research project

Stereo Vision for Precision Dimensional Measurement

Stereo-camera calibration, image processing, and geometric reconstruction for dimensional measurement of manufactured components.

Motivation & research questions

Robotic interaction with manufactured workpieces depends on reliable geometric measurements. This work investigates visual measurement at the intersection of robotics, computer vision, and precision manufacturing.

  • How can workpiece edges and vertices be localized precisely in stereo images?
  • How do calibration and reconstruction errors affect dimensional measurements?
  • How can visual measurements be expressed consistently in camera, robot, and world coordinates?

Technical methods

  • Stereo camera calibration and high-resolution image processing.
  • Detection of workpiece boundaries, edges, and vertices.
  • Subpixel feature localization and geometric reconstruction.
  • Coordinate transformations between camera, robot, and world frames.
  • Measurement-error analysis and integration of visual information with a five-degree-of-freedom parallel robotic system.

Research context & tools

This work relates to robotics and computer vision research at Tsinghua University. The technical background includes high-resolution stereo cameras, visual measurement of machined workpieces, and a five-degree-of-freedom parallel robotic system.
