Memory-Augmented Reinforcement Learning for Locomotion

An ongoing investigation into memory-based reinforcement learning policies that use historical experience to adapt across locomotion trials.

Motivation & research questions

I’m interested in whether a robot can use memory of earlier interactions to adapt its behavior without conventional parameter updates during deployment.

  • Can temporal memory help a locomotion policy adapt across multiple trials?
  • Which parts of historical experience are useful for adaptation and generalization?
  • How can transformer-inspired memory be incorporated into reinforcement learning policies?

Methods under investigation

  • Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for learning locomotion behaviors in simulation.
  • Transformer-XL-style temporal memory for retaining historical context.
  • Reinforcement learning across multiple trials.
  • Policy adaptation informed by prior observations and interactions.

Research stack

PPO, transformer-inspired policy architectures, temporal memory, and simulated locomotion. This investigation sits within my current exploration of reinforcement learning and PyTorch; a specific robot platform and simulator are not specified here.

Progress & limitations

This is ongoing research. Improved adaptation, generalization, or data efficiency has not been established here as an experimental result.

Adaptation without deployment-time parameter updates is a research objective. Comparative results and validated benchmarks will be added when available.

Code, training details, learning curves, and demonstrations will be added when available.
