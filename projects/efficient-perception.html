Exploratory

Visual Perception for Efficient Robotic Decision-Making

An exploratory direction examining when visual uncertainty matters enough to a robot’s next action to justify a perception update.

Motivation & research questions

Robotic perception may not need to process every observation with the same level of detail. I’m interested in how visual processing could respond to changes that matter for physical interaction.

  • When does a change in object geometry or scene state require a new visual estimate?
  • How can a robot estimate whether its perception is reliable enough for the next action?
  • Can selective visual guidance reduce redundant computation?
  • How does unreliable visual grounding lead to wrong-object manipulation?

Topics under investigation

  • Meaningful changes in object geometry and scene state.
  • Perception reliability and action-relevant visual uncertainty.
  • Selective visual guidance during manipulation.
  • Object grounding failures and their consequences for control.
  • The balance between visual processing cost and reliable decision-making.

Methods & tools

This direction connects RGB and RGB-D perception, object detection and segmentation, visual grounding, and robotic control. A specific implementation, perception model, and robot platform have not been established here.

Status & limitations

This is an active research question rather than a completed method.

Reduced computation and more reliable manipulation are intended goals. No measured efficiency gain, reliability improvement, or validated update strategy is claimed.

Project materials

Code, evaluation protocols, and demonstrations will be added as the research direction develops.
